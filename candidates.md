# Candidates — revision v3 (MESSAGE 1)

## Problem with v2 (goats / solar grazing, 189 chars)
- Luna (5/8/4): solar grazing is already a real, familiar practice — not absurd at
  first glance; "lacks a distinctive hook".
- Nemotron (6/9/5): "reads like a grant proposal; list of features, not a sticky
  hook that survives 1,700 pairwise votes". Money logic strong (9/10), memorability weak.
- Fix direction: payer first + 3 drivers + closing punchline, AND a premise that is
  NOT already a familiar practice. The first-glance reaction must be "that's ridiculous",
  the second-glance reaction "oh, that actually makes money".

## Candidate A — CROWS (chosen)
> We train crows to collect cigarette butts for cities, who pay per kilo because crows find butts sweepers miss, never call in sick, and work for peanuts.

- 160 chars, 1 sentence. Payer first (cities, per kilo). Three drivers: find butts
  sweepers miss / never call in sick / work for peanuts.
- Punchline is the hook: "work for peanuts" is both the idiom and literally true —
  the workforce is paid in peanuts. Sticky in pairwise voting.
- Real precedent: Corvid Cleaning, Södertälje, Sweden (2022, widely reported) —
  wild crows trained to drop cigarette butts into a machine in exchange for food;
  the city pays because butt litter is expensive to clean by hand.
- Why it beats goats: nobody's first thought is "hire birds as street cleaners";
  the absurdity is the labour force itself, not a known farming practice.

## Candidate B — MAGGOTS (backup)
> We breed maggots for hospitals, who pay per treatment because maggots eat only dead tissue, clean wounds faster than surgery, and need no anaesthesia.

- 150 chars, 1 sentence. Payer: hospitals, per treatment. Real: maggot debridement
  therapy, FDA-cleared since 2004.
- Strong gross-out hook, but the punchline is weaker than A's ("need no anaesthesia"
  vs "work for peanuts"), and the money logic is one-sided (saves surgery cost)
  rather than a per-unit price the payer already budgets for.

## Candidate C — BLACK SOLDIER FLY (backup)
> We feed restaurant scraps to maggots and sell the maggots to fish farms, who pay per kilo because maggot protein undercuts soy feed, while restaurants pay us less than landfill fees.

- 182 chars, 1 sentence. Two payers (fish farms + restaurants), double revenue —
  the strongest pure money logic of the three. Real: black soldier fly larvae
  waste-to-feed is a live industry.
- Weaker hook than A: "feed waste to maggots, sell maggots" is a process description,
  no punchline, and "maggot protein undercuts soy feed" reads like a grant proposal
  again — the exact failure mode Nemotron flagged in v2.

## Candidate D — LEECHES (rejected)
> We farm leeches for hospitals, who pay per leech because one leech can save a reattached finger that surgery alone cannot, for less than the cost of a sandwich.

- 160 chars, 1 sentence. Real (hirudotherapy, FDA-cleared 2004). Hook is good
  ("farm leeches"), but "save a reattached finger that surgery alone cannot"
  overclaims — leeches assist microsurgery, they don't replace it — and the
  sandwich punchline lands softer than "work for peanuts".

## Decision
A wins: only candidate where the punchline IS the business model (the workforce
literally works for peanuts), payer + per-unit price are concrete, and the premise
is genuinely unfamiliar to a general judge — unlike solar grazing.
